who we are

Brand overview

A home bakery run with genuine care, not a factory line. Every guideline below exists to protect one feeling: that opening a box from Puffed Postcard should feel like a gift from someone who knows you, not a purchase from a shop.

We are a passionate bakery dedicated to crafting fresh, high-quality breads, cakes, and pastries every day, using the finest ingredients to bring flavours that feel like home. Every bite is made with care and passion — freshly baked at home, with love.

— the Puffed Postcard promise

Personality

If Puffed Postcard were a person, she'd be the friend who remembers your birthday, hand-writes the card, and somehow makes the cake taste like a hug. Warm, unhurried, a little romantic — never slick or corporate.

the mark

Logo

The jelly-mould silhouette dressed in cherry-blossom florals is the one piece of Puffed Postcard artwork that never changes. It should always appear exactly as supplied — never recoloured, redrawn, or stretched.

On cream (default)
On blush
Reversed, on plum or photos
Puffed Postcard
Wordmark alone — text only, no plate

DO

  • Keep clear space around the mark of at least the height of the flower cluster on any side.
  • Use the reversed (white) version on photos, the plum panel, or any busy background.
  • Let it sit at a comfortable, readable size — it's a signature, not a background pattern.

DON'T

  • Don't recolour the florals to match a campaign — they stay rose and plum, always.
  • Don't place the unreversed mark on dark or busy photos where it disappears.
  • Don't add drop shadows, outlines, or stretch it off its natural square proportion.
the palette

Colour

The palette comes from the logo, not a template: dusty rose and plum from the florals, warmed up with a cream base and a small pour of gold for moments that deserve a little sparkle.

Cream#FBF3ECBase background — the "plate" everything sits on.
Blush#F3D9DFSoft section backgrounds, card fills.
Rose#B15C74Primary brand colour — links, icons, highlights.
Rose Deep#8C4058Hover / pressed states for rose elements.
Plum#4E1D31Headlines, primary buttons, footer.
Gold#C08A3ERare accent — prices, a single sparkle. Never a background.

Rule of thumb: a page should read as cream and rose from across the room, with plum for weight and gold used like a pinch of edible glitter — a little goes a long way.

Do

  • Use the script for the wordmark, one hero moment, or a single closing line — never body copy.
  • Set Fraunces headlines in sentence case, not ALL CAPS.
  • Give body text room: 1.6+ line height, comfortable paragraph width.

Don't

  • Don't set whole paragraphs in the script — it's decorative, not readable at length.
  • Don't introduce a fourth typeface for "just this one banner."
  • Don't tighten letter-spacing on Fraunces to save space; let headlines breathe instead.

how we sound

Voice & tone

We write the way we'd talk to someone picking up their order in person: warm, plain, a little affectionate. We say "we," never "the brand." We'd rather sound like a person than a menu.

Warm, not gushing Plain words over fancy ones Short sentences Specific over generic

DOESN'T SOUND LIKE US

"Our organization strives to deliver premium bakery solutions with a 48-hour lead time."

"Custom cake in mind? Send us a photo on WhatsApp and we'll make it happen."

putting it together

At a glance

Every piece should have

  • Cream or blush as the base — never stark white.
  • One Fraunces headline doing the talking.
  • Rose or plum for anything actionable (a button, a link, a price).
  • At most one sparkle of gold and one script moment.

Ask before it ships

  • Does this still feel handmade, or has it started to feel corporate?
  • Would Akshita or Tanvi actually say this out loud?
  • Is the logo untouched and given room to breathe?
